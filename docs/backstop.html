A refusal you can check

Retry budgets, decline-code rules, contact caps, quiet hours, value thresholds and stopping rules are code, not prompts. Every action an LLM proposes is ruled on before it can execute, and a vetoed action is logged with the rule that fired.

That turns “zero policy violations” from an assertion into a statement about a record. It also survives the thing most agent demos quietly depend on — the model being good. Backstop’s guarantee does not.

Five things worth reading off those tables

It beats the realistic alternative, not a strawman

Backstop keeps ₹9.5L more than naive retry on payments while making 69% fewer charge attempts. Naive breaks 27,889 rules across the three surfaces getting its number — including 600 retries of cards reported stolen, 2,667 attempts to chase customers who explicitly revoked their mandate, 93 chases of invoices the buyer is actively disputing, and 84 sent to buyers who had already committed to a payment date.

Nobody hears from Backstop more than six times a fortnight

Not per order, not per invoice, not per mandate — per person, across all three surfaces at once. Over six seeds the policed arm’s worst 14-day burst against any one human being is exactly 6, with nobody above it, where naive reaches 15 to 18. A customer with a failed order, a lapsed mandate and two overdue invoices is one person, and every per-subject cap can be scrupulously observed while they are written to a dozen times.

The leash is close to free, and where it is not, the price is stated

The policed and unpoliced arms run identical proposed actions, so the gap between them is the cost of compliance alone. On receivables it is positive — the unpoliced arm takes ₹36,612 it cannot keep and spends more doing it. On recurring it is positive too, ₹10,792. On payments it is negative: policing costs ₹1,795 out of ₹57.7L, or 0.03%.

It holds when the model gets worse

The tables above are the weak model. gpt-oss-20b proposes visibly worse actions — 73 re-presentations of mandates whose diagnosis had already established that re-presenting cannot work, 12 re-asks of customers who revoked their authorisation, SMS dunning where email was the safe channel — and the engine refuses every one. 241 rule breaks in the unpoliced arm, 0 in the policed one. That is the whole argument: safety that does not depend on model quality.

It holds when the model is gone

The Groq quota was exhausted mid-run and every cluster returned a 429. Recovery degraded to the deterministic playbook rather than stopping: still 35,158 actions proposed, still ₹58,30,774 recovered on payments, still zero rules broken. Worth saying plainly — the fallback out-recovered the weak model by about ₹34,000. On failures with no incident to reason about, a lookup table beats a small model. The model earns its place on diagnosis and cluster strategy, and nowhere else.

The counterfactual is the hard part

Most overdue invoices are paid whether or not anybody chases them. An accounts-payable department runs on a cycle; an invoice twelve days late is usually not a collections problem, it is a Tuesday.

Of the ₹4.02 crore outstanding, ₹1.92 crore arrives on the buyer’s own cycle with no action at all and is credited to nobody. Only ₹69,06,052 is incremental — the ceiling on what any collections effort could add. A tool that dunned the whole aged debtors report and booked everything that subsequently arrived would show a spectacular recovery rate having caused almost none of it.

The same discipline runs through the other two surfaces. A third of paused mandates resume with no prompting, so an agent that mails those customers and books the resumption has measured its own postage — ground truth marks them non-incremental and they are credited nothing however well the contact is timed. And the ninety-day receivables bracket, where settlement and write-off decisions live, is handed to a person and books nothing at all. Across six seeds that bracket is worth ₹83,267–₹5,16,803 to the naive arm. It is the single largest thing policing costs, and on one seed in six it is enough for naive to come out ahead.

It executes for real

Behind one protocol sit two backends: the simulator the measurement runs on, and a Razorpay test-mode adapter that really dispatches. Orders for re-presentments, payment links for dunning and part-payment offers, authorisation links for lapsed mandates.

A dispatch is not a recovery, and the type system says so. A payment link is paid when a human opens it, hours or days later and out of band — so a live dispatch reports a fourth outcome, dispatched, and reconciles afterwards. Which is precisely why the adapter cannot run the backtest: the four-arm comparison needs a counterfactual, and reality does not offer one.

Two guardrails, written as refusals rather than options. Live keys are refused outright — this code dispatches payment links at people, and there is no override flag. Notifications are off unless asked for, because test mode really delivers.

The gate has a queue behind it

Those 430 held actions used to go nowhere. The engine produced the ruling, the ledger counted it, and the live run correctly refused to send — then dropped it. A gate with nothing behind it is a deletion.

An approval is permission from a person, not an exemption from the rules. A release re-runs all sixteen rules in the world that holds then, not the one that held when the request was raised. Time passes while a request sits: a contact budget fills, an hour becomes a quiet hour, a mandate gets revoked, an invoice goes into dispute. A queue that dispatched whatever a reviewer approved on Tuesday would be a hole in the policy engine exactly the width of the queue. So a denial still denies after a yes, and the reviewer is told which rule overtook their decision. What the yes discharges is the approval requirement itself — and nothing else, or every approved action would be immortal in the queue.

Silence is not consent. Unanswered requests expire, and expiry is recorded rather than dropped. That is what makes the backtest’s assumption — that a merchant staffs the desk — falsifiable instead of merely convenient. A merchant who does not staff it gets a pile of expired requests, not the revenue.

430held for a person
1answered, then re-ruled and dispatched
429expired — credited to nobody

Every decision names a human being; there is no auto-approve. The backtest’s staffed desk is written down as a reviewer with a stated latency and a staffing rate, deterministic per request, so an assumption is something a reader can see and argue with. And a request’s identity is the same fingerprint the adapter uses for idempotency — verb, subject, moment, channel, amount, and deliberately not the model’s wording. Re-running a plan finds the request a reviewer has already seen, and a model rewording its own justification cannot buy a second ask.

A reschedule that nobody honours is a log entry

Three of the sixteen rules exist mainly to move an action’s moment: quiet hours pushes an SMS out of the night, retry spacing refuses to re-present a card seconds after it declined, and an outage hold defers a charge until the rail recovers.

The measurement honoured those times. The live path did not — it dispatched immediately and threw the schedule away, which made RESCHEDULE decorative in the one place it mattered. An engine that moves a 22:30 SMS to 09:00, followed by an adapter that sends it at 22:30, has not protected anybody. It has produced a record saying it did.

Nothing fires before it is due. That is the guarantee, and it is the reason there is no flush() and no force argument anywhere on the scheduler: the method that skipped the wait would remove the only thing the component promises.

The rules run again when it comes due, on the queue’s reasoning — the world an action was judged in is not the world it lands in. That is also what makes lateness safe rather than merely tolerated: an action arriving at an awkward hour is pushed again then, instead of squeaking through on yesterday’s verdict.

But it cannot be re-held forever. Something rescheduled again and again is not being carefully timed, it is being chased by a blocker that is not clearing — so after three deferrals it is abandoned and recorded. An unbounded retry loop is the same failure as an unbounded contact loop. And a missed window is not a licence to fire late: past a day, an action is dropped as stale, because a retry timed for the hour after a failure is a different act three days later, and a reminder can arrive after the invoice was paid.

The walkthrough steps the clock to each moment rather than sleeping between them, and says so — the same loop with the waiting taken out. It starts from the earliest scheduled moment rather than from now, because the batch is historical: a plan drawn against it is drawn for moments that have already passed, and reading the clock any other way would report every action as arriving a week late.

Wiring this in caught a bug in the approval queue shipped an hour earlier. Letting the candidate scan run to completion — so the queue could see the whole pool rather than only what preceded one dispatch — left a loop variable describing the last candidate instead of the chosen one, so every action was being re-ruled against an unrelated subject. It surfaced as fire-time refusals naming rules that do not depend on the clock at all, which is the kind of wrongness that is only obvious because each refusal has to name the rule that produced it.

What a receiver has to refuse

Polling is the wrong shape for this. A dispatch settles when a human opens a link, so a poll either runs constantly and learns nothing or runs rarely and reports recovery long after it happened. Razorpay pushes the news instead.

A webhook receiver is the one place in this system where an outsider proposes a change to the revenue number — everything else is proposed by our own planner and disposed of by our own rules. So it is written as a list of refusals. Four deliveries, four different answers:

deliveryverdictanswer
Signed with the wrong secretrejected400
A payment link the merchant created themselves, paidunmatched200
An order recovery dispatched, paidrecovered200
The same delivery, sent againduplicate200

The second row is the counterfactual argument arriving over HTTP. A merchant’s own payment links are paid all day; that is real revenue and recovery caused none of it. A receiver that credited every payment_link.paid on the account would make exactly the error the receivables surface exists to warn about — measuring the world’s ordinary behaviour and booking it as the agent’s work. It answers 200 because it was handled correctly, and only a bad signature is a 400.

The signature is checked against the raw bytes, before parsing, always; a missing secret fails closed, because a receiver that treats “no secret configured” as “everything is authentic” is worse than one with no check at all. Deduplication runs in two layers — on the delivery, and underneath it on the dispatch, which is the thing that can be recovered once. And settlement value goes through the same call the poll uses, so a re-registered mandate is a year of billing whichever way the news arrives.

One caveat, stated rather than buried. Receiving a real delivery needs a public URL, which a walkthrough on a laptop does not have — so the sample above is signed locally. The verification, the matching and the crediting are the real ones. Only the postman is simulated.

Building it surfaced a bug that had been in the adapter since it was written. The record of what went out lived only in memory, so a process that sent a link and then died had lost its only handle on it — and a payment landing on that link could never be credited by either path. An already-sent action now looks its reference back up. Probed rather than assumed: payment links filter on their reference, orders on their receipt. Authorisation links do not come back from the invoice listing at all, so that one is stated as a gap rather than guessed at.

What these numbers are not

Recovery rates in simulate/recoverability.py are stated configuration, not measured from Razorpay traffic. No real transaction data exists for this, so the batch is generated — which is what makes the rest checkable: every incident is planted deliberately, so detection and diagnosis are scored against truth rather than against a vibe, and the detector never sees any of it.

Read the tables as a comparison under a declared world model, not as a forecast. The agent is also graded against rates deliberately different from the ones it believes, so it cannot mark its own homework.
